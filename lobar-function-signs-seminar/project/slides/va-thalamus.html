The four thalamic vascular territories

Territory (parent vessel)NucleiClinical syndrome
Tuberothalamic / polar (PCoA)Anterior nuclei, ventral anterior, part of ventral lateralAbulia, personality change, anterograde amnesia; aphasia (left) or neglect (right)
Paramedian (P1)Dorsomedial, intralaminarReduced arousal, amnesia, vertical gaze palsy; bilateral via the artery of Percheron
Inferolateral (thalamogeniculate, P2)Ventral posterolateral and posteromedial, ventral lateralPure sensory stroke, hemiataxia, later thalamic pain (Déjerine–Roussy)
Posterior choroidal (P2)Pulvinar, lateral geniculate, lateral posteriorField defects (sectoranopia), sometimes aphasia

Thalamic relays: anterior nucleus to cingulate; dorsomedial to prefrontal cortex; ventral anterior / ventral lateral to motor cortex; ventral posterior to sensory cortex; pulvinar to parieto-occipital cortex; medial geniculate to auditory cortex; lateral geniculate to visual cortex [2].

References: [2] Blumenfeld · [56] Schmahmann 2003

Schmahmann's 2003 review in Stroke is the standard summary of thalamic vascular syndromes. The relay list explains why thalamic lesions can mimic cortical (lobar) syndromes such as aphasia, neglect and frontal-type abulia.
