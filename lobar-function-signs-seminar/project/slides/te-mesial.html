Temporal lobe · Mesial and limbic

Mesial temporal syndromes

Amnesia

Bilateral hippocampal lesions cause dense anterograde amnesia. Immediate recall and procedural memory are spared (patient H.M.) [27]. Unilateral lesions cause material-specific loss: verbal on the left, non-verbal on the right.

Klüver–Bucy syndrome

Bilateral anterior temporal / amygdala lesions cause psychic blindness, hyperorality, hypermetamorphosis, placidity and altered sexual behaviour. In humans: HSV encephalitis, trauma [28].

Mesial TLE semiology

Rising epigastric aura, fear, déjà vu and olfactory "uncinate" aura, then staring and oroalimentary automatisms. Contralateral dystonic posturing lateralizes the focus [29, 33].

Interictal behaviour

Geschwind–Gastaut syndrome: hypergraphia, hyperreligiosity, altered sexuality and "viscosity" (an adhesive, over-detailed interpersonal style) [31].

References: [27] Scoville & Milner 1957 · [28] Klüver & Bucy 1939 · [29] Penfield & Perot 1963 · [31] Waxman & Geschwind 1975 · [33] Kotagal 1989

H.M. had bilateral medial temporal resection in 1953 for intractable epilepsy. That case is why bilateral mesial temporal resection is never performed and why the opposite hippocampus must be assessed before a unilateral resection.
