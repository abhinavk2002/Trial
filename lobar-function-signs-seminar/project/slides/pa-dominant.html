Parietal lobe · Dominant hemisphere

Dominant parietal syndromes

Angular gyrus

Gerstmann syndrome

  1. Finger agnosia
  2. Right–left disorientation
  3. Agraphia
  4. Acalculia

The pure tetrad is rare. Its parts dissociate, and it often comes with alexia and anomia [1, 22].

Conduction aphasia

Fluent speech with phonemic paraphasias and good comprehension, but repetition is very poor. Lesion: supramarginal gyrus / arcuate fasciculus [1, 25].

Ideomotor apraxia

Cannot pantomime a skilled act to command or imitation, yet may manage it with the real object. Affects both hands [7, 25].

Alexia with agraphia

Acquired illiteracy: cannot read or write, from an angular gyrus lesion (Déjerine) [1].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [7] Heilman & Valenstein · [22] Gerstmann 1940 · [25] Geschwind 1965

Contrast alexia WITH agraphia (angular gyrus, parietal) with alexia WITHOUT agraphia (left occipital plus splenium), covered in the occipital section. Test apraxia with transitive gestures ("show me how you comb your hair") and intransitive ones ("wave goodbye").
