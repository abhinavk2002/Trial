Neurosurgical application (2)

From bedside signs to the operating table

Before surgery

Record a full lobar examination as the baseline. Establish language and memory dominance with the Wada test [40], fMRI, and DTI of the corticospinal tract, arcuate fasciculus and optic radiation.

Cortical stimulation

Positive motor mapping follows Penfield [12]. In awake language mapping, essential sites were discrete and varied widely between patients (Ojemann, n = 117) [42]. Resection margins more than 1 cm from language sites reduced lasting deficits [43].

Subcortical mapping

Resect up to functional, not anatomical, boundaries. White-matter tracts set the limits of resection, and slow-growing gliomas allow functional reorganization [44].

After surgery

Predict and explain deficits: transient SMA syndrome [17], quadrantanopia after temporal resection [32], neglect after right parietal surgery [23].

References: [12] Penfield & Boldrey · [17] Laplane · [23] Mesulam · [32] Ebeling · [40] Wada · [42] Ojemann · [43] Haglund · [44] Duffau

Close by repeating that anatomy predicts function only on average. Individual variation (Ojemann) and plasticity (Duffau) are the reasons for mapping rather than relying on landmarks alone.
