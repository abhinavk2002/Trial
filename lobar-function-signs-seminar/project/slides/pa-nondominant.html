Parietal lobe · Non-dominant hemisphere

Non-dominant parietal syndromes

Hemispatial neglect

Failure to attend to contralateral space. It is more frequent and severe after right lesions because the right hemisphere attends to both hemispaces. The attention network spans posterior parietal cortex, the frontal eye field and the cingulate [23].

Anosognosia and body schema

Denial of hemiplegia (anosognosia, Babinski), indifference to it (anosodiaphoria), and asomatognosia, where the limb is not recognized as one's own [1, 6].

Constructional and dressing apraxia

Right-sided lesions: fragmented copies that lose spatial relations and leave out the left side. Left-sided lesions: simplified copies that keep the overall shape [1, 7].

Topographical disorientation

Loses the way in familiar surroundings and cannot describe or draw routes or maps. Motor impersistence is also common [1, 6].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [6] Mesulam · [7] Heilman & Valenstein · [23] Mesulam 1981

Neglect and anosognosia are easily missed in a patient who is talking normally. Ask the patient to bisect a line, cancel targets and draw a clock. Neglect hampers rehabilitation and should be documented before surgery on the right parietal lobe.
