Vascular · Brainstem (3)

Medullary syndromes and the vertebral artery

Medial medullary (Déjerine)

  • Pyramid: contralateral hemiparesis
  • Medial lemniscus: contralateral loss of position and vibration sense
  • XII: ipsilateral tongue weakness
  • Vertebral artery or anterior spinal branches

Lateral medullary (Wallenberg)

  • Spinothalamic tract: contralateral body pain and temperature loss
  • Spinal V nucleus: ipsilateral facial pain and temperature loss
  • Sympathetics: ipsilateral Horner
  • Inferior cerebellar peduncle: ipsilateral ataxia
  • Vestibular nuclei: vertigo, nystagmus. Nucleus ambiguus (IX, X): dysphagia, hoarseness
  • Vertebral artery more often than PICA

Also

Hemiplegia cruciata: ipsilateral arm and contralateral leg weakness from a lesion at the pyramidal decussation.

Vertebral disease: V1/V4 atherosclerosis; V2–V3 dissection, FMD, osteophytes, Takayasu; subclavian steal.

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [4] Brazis

Mnemonic from the source seminar: medial medullary is the "4 M" structures (medial lemniscus, motor nerve XII, motor pyramidal tract, and the MLF, which can cause nystagmus); lateral medullary is the "4 S" structures (spinothalamic, spinal V, sympathetic, spinocerebellar) plus sensory nerves V and IX–X. Internuclear ophthalmoplegia is a pontine or midbrain MLF sign rather than a typical medullary one.
