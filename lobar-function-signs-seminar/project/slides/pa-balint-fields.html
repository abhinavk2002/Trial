Parietal lobe · Visual and oculomotor

Bálint syndrome, field defects and OKN

Bilateral parieto-occipital

Bálint syndrome

  • Simultanagnosia: sees only one object at a time
  • Optic ataxia: misreaches for objects under visual guidance
  • Ocular apraxia: cannot direct gaze voluntarily ("psychic paralysis of gaze")

Causes include watershed infarction and posterior cortical atrophy [1, 24].

Inferior quadrantanopia

Contralateral inferior homonymous quadrantanopia ("pie on the floor") from damage to the upper fibres of the optic radiation [4].

Optokinetic asymmetry

With a deep parietal lesion, the OKN response is reduced when the drum turns toward the side of the lesion. With a pure occipital hemianopia, OKN is symmetrical. This is Cogan's rule [1, 4].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [4] Brazis · [24] Bálint 1909

Bálint described the syndrome in 1909. Because a hemianopia plus abnormal OKN points to a deep parietal lesion, this cheap bedside test helps separate a parietal mass from an occipital infarct.
