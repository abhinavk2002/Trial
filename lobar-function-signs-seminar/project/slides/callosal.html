Disconnection syndromes

LesionSigns
Anterior callosum (ACA territory)Left-hand apraxia to verbal command, left-hand agraphia, left tactile anomia (cannot name an object held in the left hand but can pick it out)
Splenium + left occipital (PCA)Pure alexia (alexia without agraphia)
Medial frontal + anterior callosumFrontal alien hand: reflexive grasping and groping by the dominant hand
Callosal bodyCallosal alien hand: intermanual conflict, usually in the non-dominant hand
After callosotomyAcute disconnection syndrome, transient mutism, left-hand apraxia

Blood supply links these syndromes to their arteries: the ACA (pericallosal) supplies the callosum except the splenium, which is supplied by the PCA [2].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [8] Youmans & Winn · [25] Geschwind 1965 · [26] Geschwind & Kaplan 1962

Geschwind and Kaplan (1962) described a patient with a left ACA infarct involving the callosum who could not name objects in the left hand or write with it. Their report founded the modern study of disconnection syndromes.
