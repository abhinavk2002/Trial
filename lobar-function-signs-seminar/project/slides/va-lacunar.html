Classic lacunar syndromes

SyndromeClinical pictureUsual site
Pure motor hemiparesisFace, arm and leg weakness; no cortical signsPosterior limb of the internal capsule, corona radiata, basis pontis
Pure sensory strokeHemisensory lossVentral posterolateral thalamus
Sensorimotor strokeCombined weakness and sensory lossThalamus and adjacent internal capsule
Dysarthria–clumsy handDysarthria, facial weakness, clumsy handBasis pontis, genu of the internal capsule
Ataxic hemiparesisWeakness with ataxia out of proportion, same sideBasis pontis, posterior limb of the internal capsule, corona radiata

The absence of cortical signs (aphasia, neglect, field defect, gaze deviation, cortical sensory loss) is what separates a lacune from a cortical branch occlusion. This is where lobar signs pay off at the bedside [1, 55].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [55] Fisher 1982

C. Miller Fisher defined the lacunar syndromes through clinicopathological correlation; they are caused by occlusion of single penetrating arteries (lenticulostriate, thalamoperforating, pontine paramedian), usually from hypertensive lipohyalinosis.
