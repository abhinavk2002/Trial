Vascular · Internal carotid artery

ICA: branches and clinical syndromes

Intradural branches

  1. Ophthalmic artery
  2. Posterior communicating artery
  3. Anterior choroidal artery
  4. Anterior cerebral artery
  5. Middle cerebral artery

ICA stenosis or occlusion

May be silent if collaterals are good. Otherwise: recurrent TIAs; transient monocular blindness (amaurosis fugax, ophthalmic artery); MCA-territory stroke, plus ACA territory if the ACoA is poor; watershed infarcts [1].

Carotid dissection

Ipsilateral headache or neck pain with a partial Horner syndrome, with or without hemispheric ischaemia [1].

Anterior choroidal syndrome

Contralateral hemiplegia, hemisensory loss and homonymous hemianopia [1, 2].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [57] Rhoton 2002

Neurosurgical point: the anterior choroidal artery must be identified and preserved when clipping PCoA and AChA aneurysms and during mesial temporal resection, because its occlusion causes a dense capsular hemiplegia.
