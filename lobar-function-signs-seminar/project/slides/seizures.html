Seizure semiology by lobe

LobeTypical featuresLateralizing clue
FrontalBrief, frequent, nocturnal clusters; hypermotor automatisms; little post-ictal confusion. SMA: asymmetric tonic "fencing" posture with awareness kept [10]Forced head and eye version away from the focus, late in the seizure [34]
TemporalEpigastric aura, fear, déjà vu; staring, oroalimentary automatisms; longer, with post-ictal confusion [1, 29]Contralateral dystonic posturing [33]; post-ictal aphasia points to the dominant side [1]
ParietalTingling or a Jacksonian sensory march; distorted body image; vertigo [1]Sensory symptoms contralateral
OccipitalElementary coloured hallucinations, ictal blindness, eye deviation, forced blinking [1]Hallucinations in the contralateral hemifield
InsularLaryngeal constriction, perioral or widespread paraesthesias, dysarthria [39]Can mimic temporal or frontal seizures

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [10] Penfield & Jasper · [29] Penfield & Perot · [33] Kotagal 1989 · [34] Wyllie 1986 · [39] Isnard 2004

The first symptom of a seizure marks the cortex it starts in, so ask the patient and witnesses what happened first. Wyllie and colleagues (1986) showed that forced, sustained head and eye turning is reliably contralateral to the focus; unforced, early head turning is not.
