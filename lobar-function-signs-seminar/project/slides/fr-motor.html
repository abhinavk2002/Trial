Frontal lobe · Motor cortex

Primary motor cortex signs

  • Contralateral weakness with UMN signs. Distal, skilled finger movements are hit hardest [1].
  • The pattern follows the homunculus [12]. Lateral convexity (MCA) gives face and arm worse than leg. Medial paracentral cortex (ACA, parasagittal mass) gives leg worse than arm.
  • Cortical lesions tend to cause partial (monoparetic) weakness. A capsular lesion gives dense face–arm–leg hemiplegia [4].
  • Irritative signs: focal motor seizures with a Jacksonian march along the homunculus, then post-ictal Todd's paresis [1].

Surgical pearl

Bilateral parasagittal lesions, such as a falx meningioma or superior sagittal sinus thrombosis, can cause spastic paraparesis and urinary incontinence that mimic a spinal cord lesion. If the cord imaging is normal, image the head [1, 4].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [4] Brazis · [12] Penfield & Boldrey 1937

Penfield and Boldrey mapped the motor and sensory homunculus with cortical stimulation in 1937. Leg and foot sit on the medial surface, hand and face on the lateral convexity, and the face lowest, next to the Sylvian fissure.
