Temporal lobe · Functions and testing

Lateral vs medial; dominant vs non-dominant

Lateral temporal lobe

Language comprehension (area 22), naming, hearing (Heschl's gyrus), auditory–visuospatial integration

Medial temporal lobe

Memory, emotion, behaviour, sexuality, smell and taste. Also the most epileptogenic cortex.

DominantNon-dominant
Auditory perception of speechAuditory perception of non-verbal sound and music
Verbal memoryNon-verbal (visual) memory
Sensory (Wernicke) aphasiaAmusia, receptive aprosodia
Smell and taste (bilateral representation)Smell and taste (bilateral representation)

Bedside memory testing: immediate recall (digit span), recent recall (3–4 words at 5 min, 5 hidden objects), remote memory (personal and historical events) [47].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [27] Scoville & Milner 1957 · [30] Ross 1981 · [47] Strub & Black

Material-specific memory loss after unilateral temporal lobectomy is well established: verbal memory falls after left resection and non-verbal memory after right resection. This drives pre-operative neuropsychology and Wada testing.
