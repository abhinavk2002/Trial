Fibres are tightly packed here, so a small capsular lesion produces a dense contralateral face–arm–leg hemiplegia. A lateral lenticulostriate branch was called Charcot's "artery of cerebral haemorrhage" [1].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [9] Duus

Compare with cortical lesions: a lesion in the motor cortex usually spares part of the limb (monoparesis), while a capsular lesion affects face, arm and leg equally. That is the basis of the "cortical vs subcortical" clinical distinction.
