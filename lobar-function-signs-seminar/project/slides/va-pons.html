Vascular · Brainstem (2)

Pontine syndromes

SyndromeSigns (lesion)
Millard–Gubler (ventral)Ipsilateral VI and VII palsy, contralateral hemiplegia (basis pontis + VI, VII fascicles)
Raymond (ventral)Ipsilateral VI palsy, contralateral hemiplegia
Foville (dorsal)Ipsilateral horizontal gaze palsy (PPRF / VI nucleus), ipsilateral VII, contralateral hemiplegia
Facial colliculusVI nucleus + VII genu: ipsilateral gaze palsy and LMN facial palsy. Adding MLF damage gives "one-and-a-half" (8½) syndrome
Locked-inBilateral ventral pons (basilar): quadriplegia and anarthria with vertical eye movements, blinking and awareness preserved
Lateral pontine (AICA)Ipsilateral deafness, VII palsy, facial sensory loss, Horner, ataxia; contralateral pain and temperature loss

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [4] Brazis

Gaze palsy direction: a pontine lesion turns the eyes toward the hemiparesis (away from the lesion), unlike a frontal eye field lesion, which turns them toward the lesion. The "Bisguard syndrome" named in some notes is not a recognized eponym; VII plus contralateral hemiplegia is covered by Millard–Gubler.
