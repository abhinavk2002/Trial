Frontal lobe · Language

Expressive language: Broca and its neighbours

Broca's aphasia (dominant inferior frontal gyrus, areas 44/45)

  • Non-fluent, effortful, telegraphic and agrammatic speech.
  • Comprehension relatively preserved. Syntax-dependent sentences are understood poorly.
  • Repetition and naming impaired. Writing parallels speech.
  • The patient is aware and frustrated. Often comes with right face–arm weakness (superior MCA division).
  • Persistent Broca's aphasia needs a larger lesion: operculum, insula and the underlying white matter [1].

Transcortical motor aphasia

Non-fluent, but repetition is preserved. The lesion sits anterior or superior to Broca's area (SMA, ACA–MCA watershed) [1, 7].

Non-dominant homologue

Motor aprosodia: flat, monotonous speech that cannot carry emotional tone, with comprehension of prosody intact [30].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [7] Heilman & Valenstein · [30] Ross 1981

Broca described the lesion in 1861. Modern series show that a lesion confined to Broca's area causes only a transient deficit; the full chronic syndrome needs extension into the insula and periventricular white matter (Adams & Victor). Apraxia of speech is covered on the insula slide.
