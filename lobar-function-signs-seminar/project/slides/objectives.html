Introduction

Learning objectives

  1. Recall the functional anatomy of each lobe, including the main Brodmann areas and vascular territories.
  2. Recognize the deficit (negative) and irritative (positive) signs of frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital and insular lesions.
  3. Tell dominant-hemisphere syndromes apart from non-dominant ones, and spot disconnection syndromes.
  4. Examine higher cortical function at the bedside in a structured way.
  5. Map arterial territories and identify the vessel behind a cortical, lacunar or brainstem syndrome.
  6. Use lobar localization in neurosurgical practice: grading eloquence, pre-operative lateralization, intra-operative mapping and counselling about post-operative deficits.

Framework adapted from [1] Adams & Victor, [2] Blumenfeld, [4] Brazis et al.

The seminar runs lobe by lobe, then pulls the material together across lobes (insula, callosum, aphasia, seizures) and finishes with its neurosurgical use.
