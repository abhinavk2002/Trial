Introduction

Why lobar signs matter to a neurosurgeon

01

Localize

The deficit pattern points to a lobe and hemisphere before imaging. It also exposes false-localizing and "silent-area" lesions, such as right frontal or anterior temporal masses [1, 4].

02

Plan

Eloquence is built into surgical risk grading: Spetzler–Martin for AVMs and Sawaya grades for tumours. Both predict post-operative deficit [45, 46].

03

Protect

Knowing what each region does tells you what to map (motor, language, vision) and lets you predict and explain the transient and permanent deficits that follow surgery [8, 44].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [4] Brazis · [8] Youmans & Winn · [44] Duffau 2005 · [45] Sawaya 1998 · [46] Spetzler & Martin 1986

Make the point that clinical localization has not been replaced by MRI. It sets the baseline examination, it flags deficits that imaging cannot show (neglect, dysexecutive syndrome), and it decides the mapping strategy.
