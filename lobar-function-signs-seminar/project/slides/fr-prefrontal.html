Frontal lobe · Prefrontal cortex

The three prefrontal syndromes

Dorsolateral: dysexecutive

  • Poor planning, organization and set-shifting
  • Perseveration
  • Reduced verbal and design fluency
  • Impaired working memory and abstraction (concrete thinking)
  • Faulty motor programming (Luria sequences)

Orbitofrontal: disinhibited

  • Impulsivity, tactlessness, poor judgment
  • Emotional lability, irritability
  • Facetious humour (Witzelsucht)
  • Imitation and utilization behaviour [18]
  • Anosmia points to an olfactory-groove mass

Medial / cingulate: apathetic

  • Abulia: loss of drive and spontaneity
  • Psychomotor slowing
  • Akinetic mutism with bilateral lesions
  • Urinary incontinence without concern [19]
  • Leg weakness if the paracentral lobule is involved

These behaviours run on parallel frontal–subcortical circuits, so caudate, pallidal or thalamic lesions can reproduce "frontal" syndromes [15, 16].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [6] Mesulam · [15] Alexander et al. 1986 · [16] Cummings 1993 · [18] Lhermitte 1983 · [19] Andrew & Nathan 1964

Cummings (1993) described the three syndromes on the basis of the circuits of Alexander, DeLong and Strick (1986). Real patients are usually mixed. Large right frontal tumours can reach a big size with only subtle personality change, so ask the family.
