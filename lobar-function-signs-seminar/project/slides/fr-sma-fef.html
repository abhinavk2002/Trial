Frontal lobe · Premotor & eye fields

Supplementary motor area and frontal eye field

SMA (medial area 6)

  • Contralateral akinesia: little spontaneous movement despite near-normal strength on effort.
  • Speech arrest or mutism when the dominant SMA is involved.
  • After SMA resection the deficit typically resolves over days to weeks (Laplane 1977). This separates it from motor-cortex injury and should shape post-op counselling [17].
  • Seizures: asymmetric tonic ("fencing") posture, often with awareness preserved [10].

Frontal eye field (area 8)

  • Destructive lesion: conjugate deviation toward the lesion, away from the hemiparesis. It is usually transient [1].
  • Irritative focus: eyes and head turn away from the focus (contralateral version) [34].
  • The deviation is supranuclear, so the oculocephalic reflex overcomes it. A pontine lesion turns the eyes toward the hemiparesis instead [3, 4].
  • Impaired anti-saccades and motor impersistence [1].

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [3] DeJong · [4] Brazis · [10] Penfield & Jasper · [17] Laplane et al. 1977 · [34] Wyllie et al. 1986

Mnemonic: in a frontal stroke the eyes look at the lesion; in a frontal seizure they look away from it; in a pontine stroke they look at the weak side. Premotor (lateral 6) lesions produce limb-kinetic apraxia and difficulty with sensory-guided movement sequences [1, 5].
