Integration · Insula

The insula: "fifth lobe"

Interoception and taste

Primary gustatory and visceral sensory cortex; integrates bodily states with emotion [5, 6].

Autonomic control

During epilepsy surgery, stimulating the left insula more often caused bradycardia and depressor responses; the right insula, tachycardia and pressor responses [38].

Speech

Dominant precentral insula has been linked to apraxia of speech (Dronkers), although this role is debated [37].

Insular seizures

Laryngeal constriction, breathlessness, unpleasant paraesthesias and dysarthria. They can mimic temporal or frontal seizures [39].

References: [5] Kandel · [6] Mesulam · [8] Youmans & Winn · [37] Dronkers 1996 · [38] Oppenheimer et al. 1992 · [39] Isnard et al. 2004

Surgical point: insular gliomas sit next to the lateral lenticulostriate arteries and the M2 branches. Injury to these vessels, not to insular cortex itself, causes most permanent hemiparesis after insular resection [8].
