Vascular · Overview

From the aortic arch to the circle of Willis

Anterior circulation

Brachiocephalic trunk (right) and arch (left) give the common carotids, then the internal carotid arteries (ICA). The ICA gives the ophthalmic, posterior communicating and anterior choroidal arteries, then ends as the ACA and MCA [2].

Posterior circulation

Vertebral arteries arise from the subclavians. V1 runs from the origin to C6 foramen; V2 through the foramina C6–C2; V3 from C2 around C1; V4 is intradural. They give PICA, then join to form the basilar artery (AICA, SCA), which ends as the PCAs [2, 4].

Circle of Willis

A1 segments plus the ACoA in front; the ICAs; PCoAs; P1 segments behind. It is the main collateral route, but is complete in only a minority of people [2, 57].

References: [2] Blumenfeld · [4] Brazis · [57] Rhoton 2002 (supratentorial arteries)

Leptomeningeal anastomoses between the ACA, MCA and PCA at the convexity create the watershed (border-zone) territories. These explain the transcortical aphasias and Bálint syndrome after hypoperfusion.
