Classification of limb apraxia

TypeDefectUsual lesion
IdeationalCannot sequence a multi-step act, such as making teaLeft temporoparietal or diffuse
ConceptualLoses tool–action knowledge (content errors, tool misuse)Left parietotemporal, diffuse
Ideomotor, parietal typeSpatial and timing errors on pantomime; cannot recognize gesturesLeft inferior parietal lobule
Ideomotor, disconnection typeRecognizes gestures but cannot produce themParietal–premotor/SMA connections
CallosalApraxia of the left hand to verbal commandAnterior corpus callosum
DissociationFails in one input modality only (verbal, visual or tactile)Callosal or intrahemispheric disconnection
Limb-kineticLoss of fine, independent finger movementsContralateral premotor / sensorimotor cortex

References: [7] Heilman & Valenstein · [25] Geschwind 1965 · [26] Geschwind & Kaplan 1962 · [1] Adams & Victor

Examine in order: pantomime to command, imitation, then use of the real object. Testing both hands separately unmasks callosal apraxia. Liepmann's model is the basis of this scheme: the left parietal lobe stores movement formulas and sends them forward to premotor cortex and across the callosum.
