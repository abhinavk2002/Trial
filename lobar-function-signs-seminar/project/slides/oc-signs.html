Occipital lesion signs

SignFeatures and lesion
Homonymous hemianopiaContralateral and congruous, often with macular sparing (dual PCA/MCA supply to the pole). Pupils are spared.
QuadrantanopiaCuneus lesion gives an inferior defect; lingual gyrus lesion gives a superior defect
Cortical blindnessBilateral V1 lesions: no vision, normal pupils and fundi
Anton syndromeCortical blindness denied, with confabulation (visual anosognosia)
Riddoch phenomenonMoving stimuli still seen in the blind field
Achromatopsia; akinetopsiaLoss of colour vision (V4, inferior occipitotemporal); loss of motion vision (bilateral V5/MT) [36]
Visual inattentionVisual extinction and neglect of the contralateral field
Irritative signsElementary hallucinations (flashes, coloured shapes) in the contralateral field; palinopsia, polyopia

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [4] Brazis · [36] Zihl et al. 1983

"Gun-barrel" (tubular) vision can follow bilateral PCA infarction with macular sparing on both sides. Pupillary reflexes stay normal because the pupillary pathway leaves the optic tract before the lateral geniculate body.
