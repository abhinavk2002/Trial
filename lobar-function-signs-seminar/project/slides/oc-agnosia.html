Occipital lobe · Higher visual disorders

Visual agnosias and pure alexia

Visual object agnosia

Apperceptive type (cannot form a percept or copy) or associative type (can copy but cannot name or use). Usually bilateral occipitotemporal [1].

Prosopagnosia

Cannot recognize familiar faces but recognizes people by voice. Bilateral or right fusiform lesion [1, 2].

Loss of visual memory

Tested by hiding 5 objects and asking for their locations later [47].

Left PCA: occipital cortex + splenium

Alexia without agraphia

  • Cannot read, even words they wrote themselves
  • Writing and spelling preserved; reads letter by letter
  • Right homonymous hemianopia; often colour anomia
  • Mechanism: right visual cortex cut off from the left angular gyrus by the splenial lesion (Déjerine; Geschwind) [25]

References: [1] Adams & Victor · [2] Blumenfeld · [25] Geschwind 1965 · [47] Strub & Black

Pure alexia is the classic disconnection syndrome. The patient sees words with the right occipital cortex, but the information cannot cross the damaged splenium to reach the language areas in the left hemisphere.
